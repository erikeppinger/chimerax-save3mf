3MF export for 3D printing

Saves what you see as a 3MF file, the format 3D-printing software opens, and keeps your colours: as filament assignments for a multi-filament printer, or as full colour for a full-colour printer. Flavours says which to use; Printing protein models walks through an example and collects practical tips.

save

save  filename.3mf  [ models model-spec ]  [ size mm ]  [ scale mm-per-Å ]  [ colors true|false ]  [ maxColors N ]  [ flavor prusa|bambu|fullcolor ]  [ paint true|false ]  [ parts chain|model ]  [ smoothness mm|off ]  [ check true|false ]

What is shown is what gets saved: hidden atoms, parts cut away by a clipping plane and anything else not drawn are left out. The model is placed on the build plate, ready for the slicer.

  • models – which models to save; by default everything shown.
  • size – the length of the model's longest side, in millimetres. Usually what you want, since a molecule measured in Ångstroms is far too small to print.
  • scale – millimetres per Ångstrom instead (default 1). Ignored when size is given.
  • colors – keep the colours (default true), or save the shape only with false.
  • maxColors – merge similar colours until at most this many are left, typically the number of filaments your printer has. By default nothing is merged, up to the 15 colours a slicer can take. Has no effect with flavor fullcolor. 3mf palette helps choose the number.
  • flavor – which slicer or kind of printer the file is for; see Flavours.
  • paint – with a filament flavour, paint the colours onto one model (default), or with false split it into one part per colour; see Painting rather than splitting.
  • parts – with a filament flavour, one part per chain or per model, kept together in place and still in colour. By default the scene is one piece. See One part per chain.
  • smoothness – how deep the flat sides of round shapes may be in the print, in millimetres (default 0.05). Where they would be deeper, the model is saved rounder; off saves exactly what is drawn. See Smoothness.
  • check – report what the printer will make of the model (default true); see Printability report.

The model always sits at positive coordinates, its lowest point at the build plate. A slicer that finds no placement in a file takes the coordinates literally, and anything at negative x or y would be off the plate.

Saving takes a moment for big models: sticks, balls and spheres of a large structure are millions of triangles, and haemoglobin as sticks takes about half a minute. ChimeraX doesn't respond until the file is written; the status line at the bottom of the window shows what it is working on.

3mf palette

3mf palette  [ models model-spec ]  [ maxColors N ]  [ size mm ]  [ scale mm-per-Å ]  [ layerHeight mm ]  [ tools N ]  [ smoothness mm|off ]

Shows the colours a save would produce, without saving anything. On its own, it lists what merging into fewer colours would look like and what each choice costs in printing time, so you can pick the number first. ΔE measures how much the colours change, 0 meaning not at all; the words next to it say what that looks like: 

526 distinct colors across 814254 triangles; 307 layers at 0.20 mm,
about 3h 57m spent changing tools (774 changes on a 5-tool printer)
  merge to 2     ΔE 32.5   strong shift       +1h 28m changing tools
  merge to 4     ΔE 21.7   strong shift       +3h 15m changing tools
  merge to 5     ΔE 16.6   strong shift       +4h 40m changing tools
  merge to 8     ΔE 9.3    clearly different  +5h 44m changing tools
  no merge       ΔE 0.0    526 colors         +3h 57m changing tools

With maxColors N it shows that palette in detail: how much of the model each colour covers, how many layers it appears in, and the filament changes it causes. At the given size it also says whether saving will smooth the model, and how many more triangles that takes.

Similar colours are merged the way the eye judges them (in CIELAB colour space, not RGB), and large areas count for more than small ones, so a big surface is not merged away for the sake of a few ligand triangles. Each merged colour is one that actually occurs in the scene, not an average.

Print cost

A multi-colour print spends much of its time changing filament, and each change wastes some filament on cleaning. What makes a colour expensive is therefore not how much of the model it covers but how many layers it appears in, because each of those layers needs a filament change. A colour on less than one percent of the model can still cost a large share of the printing time, and the palette points that out: 

Color 5 (1a3n_D SES surface red) is 0.4% of the model but costs about 15m of
tool changes, because it appears in 52 of 269 layers. Dropping it with
maxColors 4 would get that time back.
  • The time shown is only the time spent changing filament, not the whole print, which depends on your print settings.
  • A filament change was measured at 18.4 seconds on an Original Prusa XL at 0.20 mm layers. Single-nozzle changers such as an MMU are much slower, so use the figures to compare choices, not as a quote.
  • Checked against PrusaSlicer on real files, the number of changes is within about 1% and the time within 5%.
  • Colours beyond the number of filaments your printer has cost no extra time, because they all print with filament 1. Tell the palette how many your printer has with tools N (default 5).
  • The number of layers depends on how tall the print is, so size and scale mean the same as for save, and layerHeight (default 0.2 mm) should match your print settings.

Smoothness at print size

ChimeraX draws for the screen: a support is a rod with 10 flat sides, a bond or atom a few dozen triangles, a ribbon about a dozen sides around. On screen nobody notices; printed large, the flat sides show. A 0.6 Å support printed at 200 mm has flat sides 0.17 mm inside a true circle – almost a whole 0.2 mm layer.

So every save checks how deep the flat sides would be at your print size. Where they are deeper than smoothness (0.05 mm by default, well under one layer), the model is saved with more sides. Your view in ChimeraX stays as it was. The log says so before the file is written: 

Smoothing for print. At 5.82 mm/Å the flat facets ChimeraX draws would be
up to 0.17 mm deep (supports 0.17 mm, ribbons 0.10 mm), more than the 0.05 mm
target. Exporting with supports 10 → 20 sides, ribbons 16 → 24 sides,
20 → 30 divisions (about 39,008 → 86,888 triangles). The display is
left as it is; smoothness off exports exactly what is on screen.

3mf palette with the same size shows this in advance. A smaller print often needs nothing: the same model at 100 mm is saved as drawn.

Atoms, bonds, supports and ribbons are smoothed this way. Surfaces of molecules and maps are not: how fine they are is set when ChimeraX computes them. If one is too coarse for your print size, the log says so and suggests a finer one, for example surface gridSpacing 0.3.

Flavours: which printer is this for?

Multi-filament printers and full-colour printers need different files, and the two families of filament slicers store colours in different ways, so one file cannot suit them all.

flavorForColours
prusa (default) PrusaSlicer painted, each colour assigned to a filament; up to 15
bambu Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer painted, each colour assigned to a filament; up to 15
fullcolor full-colour printers – inkjet, binder jet, PolyJet – and 3MF viewers every shade kept, blended smoothly across the surface; no limit

fullcolor suits gradients such as mlp hydrophobicity or colouring by B-factor: it keeps the colours exactly as they are. (generic is an older name for it.)

On a multi-filament printer every colour is a real filament, so there can be no more colours than the printer holds. maxColors and the palette preview are there for that. Each colour arrives already assigned to a filament, so there is nothing to assign by hand in the slicer.

Painting rather than splitting

The colours are painted onto one model, just as a slicer's own paint tool does it. The model stays in one piece, so the slicer has nothing to repair.

With paint false the model is instead split into one part per colour. That works too, and each part gets a name in the slicer's list, but every part has open edges where it met its neighbours, and the slicer warns about each of them.

A slicer can paint at most 15 colours. A scene with more, such as a rainbow or a Coulombic surface, has its similar colours merged into 15, and the log says so. Most printers hold fewer filaments than that: set maxColors to the number yours has, since colours beyond it all print with filament 1.

Slicers don't always fill a painted colour all the way through. The Original Prusa XL profiles, for example, paint each colour only 5 mm deep, around a core of a neighbouring colour: invisible from outside, but not if the model is cut open. See slicer settings for this and for beam interlocking, which holds the colours together.

One part per chain

A complex, such as an AlphaFold prediction of two interacting proteins, normally arrives in the slicer as one piece. With parts chain each chain becomes a part of its own, named chain A, chain B and so on, which you can select, hide or set up separately. parts model does the same per model (#1, #2, …, with their submodels), for structures opened side by side. Chains are counted per structure, so the models of an ensemble (#1.1, #1.2, …) each keep their own chain A.

save complex.3mf parts chain flavor bambu
  • The chains stay in place. The parts belong to one object. Separate objects would not stay put: every slicer drops each object onto the bed, and Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer also rearrange them.
  • The colours stay. Each part keeps its painted colours, such as a highlighted binding site.
  • Use the right flavour. PrusaSlicer needs flavor prusa, Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer flavor bambu. With the wrong one, Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer show a prusa file as one part without colours, and PrusaSlicer splits a bambu file into separate objects.

How the scene is divided: a molecular surface follows the atoms underneath it, a cartoon follows its residues, and each sphere, stick or support goes, whole, to the chain of the nearest atom shown – so a bond or support between two chains belongs to one of them. Anything not made of atoms, such as a map surface, goes to its model.

A surface that covers several chains, as surface with enclose makes it, is saved as one part, named after its chains (surface of chains A, B): split along the chains, it would have holes. Everything else in the scene is still split by chain. For a separate surface per chain, run these before saving:

surface close
surface

Where chains touch, their parts overlap; the slicer prints the overlap once. To print the chains as separate pieces, split the object in the slicer. That drops the colours, so give each piece its filament afterwards, or save each chain on its own.

Printability report

Unless you give check false, every save reports what the printer will make of the model. The report only describes; nothing is changed.

ChimeraX builds a scene from many separate shapes – each helix, strand and loop of a ribbon, every atom sphere, every bond – that touch or overlap rather than forming one connected surface. So the report counts what matters for printing, not how the shapes are built:

  • Printed pieces – shapes closer than about one nozzle width fuse in the print and count as one piece. Six hundred overlapping atom spheres are one object.
  • Hollow spaces – empty space sealed inside the model, such as a cavity in a protein or a density map. The slicer leaves it hollow.
  • Loose pieces – only what touches nothing else, such as a water molecule or ion floating free, is reported as printing separately.
  • Open surfaces – with their cause, usually a surface cut open by a clipping plane; slicers close them when slicing. The open ends ChimeraX leaves on bond rods are closed when saving.
  • Too thin or too big – parts thinner than a 0.4 mm nozzle can print, or a model larger than most build plates.

To make a structure printable – struts between loose parts, thicker ribbons, solvent removed – use the NIH 3D print presets. Save3MF saves and checks; it doesn't change your model. After a preset, look over the supports it added: Check the supports shows how to find and remove one that sits badly.
